What the isolation test actually shows

01

Sparsity beats acceleration.

qwen3.6-main — sparse MoE, zero speculative decoding — runs 70–74 tok/s. qwen3.6-27b-mtp — dense, with MTP actively accelerating it — tops out at 38 tok/s. Being sparse (only ~3B of 35B parameters active per token) beat every speculative-decoding trick we threw at the dense models, combined.

02

Speculative decoding's payoff depends entirely on what it's accelerating.

On the MoE model, MTP added almost nothing measurable — qwen3.6-mtp (66.5–81.4) and qwen3.6-main (70.4–74.1) overlap within noise. On the dense 27B, the same technique nearly doubled throughput (20.3 → 37.1–38.1 tok/s) with much higher draft acceptance (87–92% vs. 76–88% on the MoE model) — a slow dense backbone gives speculative decoding more room to work with.

03

Two independent quantizations, no detectable gap.

Meta ships an official 19.7GB "K-Quant-Dynamic" GGUF for Muse Glimmer, separate from Unsloth's own 14.8GB conversion of the same weights — different pipelines, ~5GB apart, for the same nominal "4-bit" tier. Tool-calling reliability, draft acceptance, and vision output were statistically indistinguishable between them in this testing. Whatever quality gap Meta's own degradation numbers claim didn't surface in real usage — it would take a proper statistical benchmark to see it, not manual comparison.

04

Every model is reliable at a generous budget. The gap only shows up when you starve it.

All six profiles hit 3/3 with a 1024-token tool-call budget. Drop that to 400 tokens and real spread appears — from 3/5 up to 5/5. Speculative decoding changes generation speed, not how long a model decides to think before acting, so a fast profile with a stingy client-side token budget can still get cut off mid-reasoning more often than a slow, unaccelerated one.

05

Vision + speculative decoding: model-dependent, not universally blocked.

The original Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-MTP model's own card states MTP and a vision projector (--mmproj) aren't supported together — confirmed, which is why it's split into two profiles here. But every dense Qwen3.6-27B-based MTP model tested — official Qwen, and a community merge — ran vision and MTP simultaneously with no conflict, and Muse Glimmer's DFlash decoder never had the restriction at all.

Method & caveats

  • Every number is a live measurement from this exact rig on the same day, not copied from a model card. Speed and draft-acceptance figures come straight from llama-server's own timings response field.
  • Small sample sizes. 3 runs per profile at the generous budget, 5 at the tight one. Enough to see real, order-of-magnitude effects (sparsity, MTP on dense) — not enough to treat single-percentage-point differences as fact.
  • Sampling params matched each model's own documented recommendation (e.g. Muse Glimmer's temp=1.0/top_p=0.95/top_k=64), not a single fixed setting forced onto every model.
  • Benchmark-card claims are self-reported by each model's publisher and not independently audited here — only what was directly tested is presented as fact.
  • Context fixed at 131,072 tokens across every profile for a fair VRAM comparison, hand-verified to actually fit — several models' own auto-tuning (--fit) picked far smaller defaults than what the hardware could actually support.
